# Generation hook: invention first, one saved composition

Compose the requested sound at full ambition. There is no quick-demo requirement, fixed instrument palette, event budget or compulsory arrangement. Spend effort on musical and sonic decisions rather than hand-writing XML. If the user asks for a dense masterpiece, develop multiple interacting ideas and contrasting textures, with a deliberate foreground and intelligible low end. Do not quietly shrink that request to a short repeated loop.

Before coding, choose a distinctive musical premise and a suitable sound palette. Think about motion at several scales: articulation within gestures, interplay within phrases, and development across the piece. Choose form from the request; a continuous transformation, fugue, evolving dance groove or through-composed scene can be as valid as a returning hook. Avoid making all voices variations of the same sine-plus-decay sound. Invent instrument behavior: excitation, body/resonance, articulation and spatial response. Match technique to desired sensation.

For music, give melodic composition its own pass before orchestration. Unless the request calls for an unpitched or deliberately nonmelodic texture, write a foreground phrase with a distinctive rhythm, contour, held notes/breaths and an intentional destination. Check it with a simple voice before surrounding it with detail. A scale walk, chord arpeggio or short transposed cell can support a melody but does not automatically supply one. Let the phrase extend across bar lines and harmony changes instead of resetting to each bass root. Establish its identity before transforming it; novelty can come from its surroundings while its important intervals and rhythm remain recognizable. Protect its audibility through register, articulation and selective space in competing parts without draining the overall density.

Use a bounded creative revision loop inside this turn: draft the phrase → inspect or audition it in isolation → hear/inspect it against the arrangement → revise the weakest link (phrase, harmonic fit, or masking). When uncertain, render a short neutral-voice melody probe and the same passage in context. Do not confuse louder lead, more notes or a successful numerical check with a better tune. Preserve the chosen phrase explicitly in the saved score so later orchestration cannot accidentally erase it. Choose phrase length and form freely; no compulsory hook, key, instrument or breakdown. For nonmusical sound design apply this attention to a recognizable gesture instead, not a forced tune.

For deeper technique when useful, read the synthesis notebook supplied by Studio. It offers independent options, not a prescribed stack. Try a short representative passage when a new timbre or dense combination is uncertain, improve it, then develop the full piece. A draft is an internal step, not the final delivery. When tools cannot audition sound, use spectral/envelope evidence and say so; do not make unsupported claims about how it sounds.

Keep one authoritative composition in the SVG. Programmatically compose → serialize → decode → synthesize. Its visible projection and audible performance must describe the same musical decisions. Arbitrary curves, fields, events, embedded parameters, procedural rules and compact patterns with repeat schedules are all valid; no universal schema or requirement to draw one rectangle per note. Python may implement elaborate instruments and pattern expansion, with composition-specific choices controlled by the saved score. Merely reading a few seed events while independently composing the rest in Python is insufficient.

Use shared timing/lane mappings for display and decoding. A readable overview plus selected detail is enough; do not simplify the sound to make a diagram manageable. Preserve the composition generator alongside the renderer when useful for future edits. Keep arrangement seeds separate from synthesis noise seeds. Visual-only revisions must preserve every musical parameter and decoded audio; musical revisions must change the intended musical material.

Before delivery, review the actual implementation against the request: are the voices meaningfully different, do parts interact, does development exceed mute/unmute layering, and does the desired scale survive? Correct a weak passage rather than simply describing it as rich. These are artistic prompts, not numerical quotas or a mandatory extra model turn.
